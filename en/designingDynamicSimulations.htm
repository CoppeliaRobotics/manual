Designing dynamic simulations

In CoppeliaSim, only a limited number of objects will be dynamically simulated. Those are shapes, joints and force sensors, but it depends on the scene structure and object properties, whether a given object is dynamically simulated. Dynamically simulated objects are easily recognized during simulation, since following icon appears next to the object's name in the scene hierarchy:

[Icon marking dynamically simulated objects]


Double-clicking the icon in the scene hierarchy (during simulation only) displays some information related to the object's dynamic behavior. Objects that are supposed to be dynamically simulated but which, for one reason or another, cannot be dynamically simulated, display following icon instead:

[Warning icon when an object cannot be dynamically simulated]


Static/non-static, respondable/non-respondable shapes

Shapes can be classified into 4 types depending on their behavior during dynamic simulation:

[Dynamic simulation main shape types]


During dynamic simulation, static shapes are not influenced (i.e. their position relative to their parent object is fixed), whereas non-static shapes are directly influenced by gravity or other constraints (e.g. dynamically enabled joints, see hereafter). See also shape.dynamic.

Respondable shapes influence each other during dynamic collision (i.e. they produce a mutual collision reaction, they bounce off each other). The following figure illustrates the static/non-static, respondable/non-respondable behaviors:

[Static/non-static, respondable/non-respondable shape behaviors and interactions]


Two respondable shapes always produce a collision reaction, unless their respective collision masks don't overlap. Static/non-static, respondable/non-respondable shape properties, as well as collision masks can be set in the shape dynamics dialog. See also shape.respondable and shape.respondableMask.

Note: in MuJoCo, static shapes (mocap bodies) do not transmit friction forces. For that reason, static shapes in MuJoCo will always be represented as a mocap body linked to a non-static free body via a weld constraint (unless shape.kinematic is set to false).


Simple constraints between shapes

Non-static shapes fall (i.e. are influenced by gravity) if they are not otherwise constrained. Dynamic constraints between shapes can be set up by attaching two shapes together via a dynamically enabled joint, a dynamically enabled force sensor, or via a dummy-dummy dynamic overlap constraint.

Rigid compounds

Two non-static shapes that are not linked through dynamically enabled joints or force sensors move independently from each other during dynamic simulation. If you want two or more shapes to behave as one single shape, you have to group them ([Edit > Shape grouping/merging > group]). Make sure you adjust the inertia matrix of the final shape appropriately. See also sim.scene:groupShapes, shape.dynamics.inertiaMatrix, shape.dynamics.centerOfMass, shape.dynamics.mass, and make sure you also read the section about primitive shapes further down:

[Compound of several non-static shapes to obtain a single non-static shape]


Compound shapes behave as rigid entities and also share identical dynamic properties (e.g. friction coefficient). Sometimes a rigid entity with differentiated dynamic properties is needed. In that case, shapes can be rigidly linked via a force sensor.


Design consideration 1

Use primitive shapes. Whenever possible, try using primitive shapes as respondable shapes: primitive shapes are much more stable and faster during dynamic simulation. Instead of using the complicated triangular mesh of a robot model as respondable shape, or its slightly better convex representation, try approximating the triangular mesh with several primitive shapes, then grouping them [Edit > Shape grouping/merging > group]. Design consideration 2

Use convex shapes instead of random shapes. When using a respondable primitive shape is not possible or practical (think of a torus for instance), you can generate a convex shape, or a convex decomposed shape. Convex shapes perform faster and are more stable than random shapes.

Select the shapes, then select [Modules > Geometry / Mesh > Convex hull...]. Refer also to the item [Modules > Geometry / Mesh > Convex decomposition]. The following figure illustrates a convex decomposition:

Design consideration 3

Carefully inspect the dynamic content of a scene. It can sometimes be a little bit confusing to work with hidden shapes meant to play an active role in the simulation. This is often the case with dynamically enabled shapes, joints or force sensors: indeed, they are most of the time hidden to the viewer's eye. The dynamic content can however always be inspected during a simulation, by activating the dynamic content visualization button:

[Dynamic content visualization button]


[Normal and "dynamic content" scene visualization]


Dynamic objects will appear in various colors, depending on their function or settings. Refer to the following figure:

[Color coding for dynamic objects (in dynamic content visualization mode)]


Notice in the above figure non-primitive shapes: they have their triangular mesh contour represented in green or grey color. Dynamically simulated non-primitive shapes should be avoided at all cost, since they require much longer calculation times, and present a not very stable behavior (convex shapes are however faster and more stable than non-convex shapes). Refer also to the design consideration 1 here above.

Design consideration 5

Carefully choose the model base object. When building a dynamic model, actually also when building a static model, always carefully consider what role the model will be playing. Will it be used on its own? Can it be built on top of another model or object? Or can it accept other models or objects to be built on top of it? Consider following example:

You have created a model of a mobile robot. You have also created a model of a gripper that you want to be able to attach to the robot model. The other way round will never make sense (attaching the robot model on top of the gripper model). You might also want to use the robot model and the gripper model on their own. The following is the view of the scene hierarchy of a possible model definition:

Notice the model icon next to the 'robot' and 'gripper' shape objects. This indicates that all objects built on top of them are part of the same model definition. The two models operate well on their own. However if you try to attach the gripper on top of the robot (by selecting the gripper model, then the robot model, then clicking [Edit > Set parent, keep pose(s)]), the gripper does not stay fixed on the robot during simulation. Design consideration 6

Use reasonable sizes. Shapes that are very long and thin or that are too small might behave strangely (jittering, jumping).

This requirement can be loosened depending on the physics engine.


Design consideration 7

Keep masses similar and not too light. When linking two shapes with a dynamically enabled joint or a dynamically enabled force sensor, make sure the two shapes' masses are not too different (m1<10*m2 and m2<10*m1), otherwise the joint or force sensor might be very soft and wobbly and present large positional/orientational errors (this effect can however also be used as a natural damping sometimes). Additionally, very low mass shapes should be avoided since they won't be able to exert large forces onto other shapes (even if propelled by high force actuators). See also shape.dynamics.mass.

This requirement can be loosened depending on the physics engine.


Design consideration 8

Keep inertia matrices relatively large. Try keeping the inertia matrix relatively large, otherwise mechanical chains might be difficult to control and/or might behave in a strange way. Refer to the shape dynamics dialog. See alsoshape.dynamics.inertiaMatrix and shape.dynamics.centerOfMass.

This requirement can be loosened depending on the physics engine.


Design consideration 9

Assign dynamic shapes to layer 9. Assign all dynamic shapes which are supposed to be hidden to layer 9 (refer to the scene object common properties): in CoppeliaSim, by default, all layers are visible, except for layers 9-16. When editing or testing a scene, you can then quickly visualize all hidden shapes in the scene by temporarily enabling layer 9 (refer also to the layer selection dialog). See also sceneObject.layer.
